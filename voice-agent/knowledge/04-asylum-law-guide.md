# German Asylum and Refugee Law — General Information Guide

IMPORTANT: General information only, not legal advice. Asylum law changes often. Since 12 June 2026 the new
EU asylum rules (GEAS reform / EU Pact on Migration and Asylum) apply in Germany, so some procedures and
deadlines may differ from older information. ALWAYS tell people to check their exact deadline in the
"Rechtsbehelfsbelehrung" at the end of their letter and to get help from an asylum counselling office
or a lawyer for migration law (Fachanwalt für Migrationsrecht) immediately.

## Main laws and offices
- Laws: Article 16a Grundgesetz, Asylgesetz (AsylG), Aufenthaltsgesetz (AufenthG), Asylbewerberleistungsgesetz (AsylbLG),
  Geneva Refugee Convention, EU asylum regulations.
- BAMF (Bundesamt für Migration und Flüchtlinge): decides on asylum applications.
- Ausländerbehörde: residence permits, Duldung, work permission, deportation matters.
- Verwaltungsgericht (administrative court): handles lawsuits (Klage) against BAMF decisions.
- Sozialamt / Leistungsbehörde: AsylbLG benefits.

## The asylum procedure step by step
1. Asking for asylum (Asylgesuch) at the border, police, Ausländerbehörde or a reception centre.
2. Registration, fingerprints, photo, security check. You get an arrival certificate (Ankunftsnachweis).
3. Distribution to a federal state (EASY system, Königsteiner Schlüssel) and a reception centre (Erstaufnahmeeinrichtung).
4. Formal asylum application (Asylantrag) at a BAMF office. You then get an Aufenthaltsgestattung
   (permission to stay during the procedure).
5. Personal interview (Anhörung). THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT STEP.
6. Written decision (Bescheid) from BAMF, delivered by post. Check the mail every day.

## The interview (Anhörung) — tips
- Tell your own story in detail, in order, with dates, places and names: what happened to YOU, why you fled,
  what would happen if you went back.
- You have the right to an interpreter in your language (e.g. Pashto, Dari, Urdu). Say immediately if you
  do not understand the interpreter well.
- Women can ask for a female interviewer and interpreter; victims of violence, torture or persecution for
  sexual orientation can ask for specially trained staff.
- Bring all documents and evidence (ID, papers, photos, medical reports, threats received).
- At the end the protocol is translated back. Correct mistakes before signing. You can ask for a copy.
- Preparing with an asylum counselling office before the interview helps a lot.
- Being late or missing the interview without good reason can lead to the case being stopped.

## Types of protection and what they give
1. Asylberechtigung (Art. 16a GG): political persecution by the state. Rarely granted alone.
2. Flüchtlingsschutz / refugee status (§3 AsylG, Geneva Convention): persecution because of race, religion,
   nationality, political opinion or social group (e.g. gender, sexual orientation).
   → Residence permit for 3 years, travel document for refugees (blue passport), privileged family reunification.
3. Subsidiärer Schutz (§4 AsylG): serious harm threatened, e.g. death penalty, torture, or danger from war/armed conflict.
   → Residence permit for 1 year, extended for 2 years. Family reunification for subsidiary protection was
   suspended by law in 2025 for two years (exceptions only for hardship cases); check current status.
4. Abschiebungsverbot (§60 Abs. 5 or 7 AufenthG): no deportation, e.g. because of serious illness or
   catastrophic living conditions in the home country.
   → Residence permit for at least 1 year.
All recognised people may work and get access to integration courses. Benefits then come from the Jobcenter.

## Negative decisions and deadlines (VERY IMPORTANT)
The deadline starts when the decision is delivered (Zustellung). Write down the date the letter arrived
and keep the envelope.
- Rejected as "unbegründet" (unfounded): Klage at the Verwaltungsgericht within 2 WEEKS.
  The Klage normally stops deportation. Departure deadline is usually 30 days.
- Rejected as "offensichtlich unbegründet" (manifestly unfounded) or "unzulässig" (e.g. another EU country
  is responsible): Klage AND an urgent application (Eilantrag, §80 Abs. 5 VwGO) within 1 WEEK.
  Without a successful Eilantrag, deportation can happen even while the Klage is pending.
- Only partial protection (e.g. subsidiary instead of refugee status): an "Aufstockungsklage" is possible,
  also within 2 weeks.
- The Klage can be filed in writing or in person at the court office (Rechtsantragsstelle). A short Klage
  can be filed first and the reasons sent later. No court fees are charged in asylum cases, but lawyers cost money;
  Prozesskostenhilfe (legal aid) can be requested.
- The exact deadline is always written in the Rechtsbehelfsbelehrung of the decision. Trust that, and act at once.

## Responsibility of other EU countries (Dublin / EU Pact)
- If fingerprints were taken in another EU country (or Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein), Germany may say
  that country is responsible and decide the application is "unzulässig". Transfer can follow.
- Since June 2026 the EU Pact replaced the Dublin III regulation with new rules (Asylum and Migration Management
  Regulation), with new screening and border procedures. Deadlines in these cases are very short — get help the same day.
- Family members in Germany, minors, and serious illness can matter. Tell the BAMF about them.

## Safe countries of origin
- People from countries listed as "sichere Herkunftsstaaten" (for example Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo,
  Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Ghana, Senegal, Georgia, Moldova; the list can change) usually get faster
  procedures and are often rejected as manifestly unfounded. They usually must stay in the reception centre and
  face work bans. They can still win if they show individual persecution.

## Life during the procedure
- Aufenthaltsgestattung: proof you may stay during the procedure. Carry it and renew it in time.
- Residence rules: obligation to live in the assigned place (Wohnsitzauflage) and, at first, restrictions on
  travelling (räumliche Beschränkung / Residenzpflicht). Ask permission before travelling.
- Benefits: Asylbewerberleistungsgesetz (AsylbLG). After 36 months in Germany, higher benefits similar to
  social assistance (Analogleistungen) are possible. Benefits can be reduced for Dublin cases or non-cooperation.
- Health care: limited during the first 36 months (urgent and pain treatment, pregnancy); a Behandlungsschein
  or health card depending on the state.
- Work: usually forbidden in the first 3 months, and longer while living in a reception centre. After that,
  work needs permission from the Ausländerbehörde. People from safe countries of origin are often banned from work.
- Children: right and duty to go to school; kindergarten access.
- Change of address must always be reported to the BAMF and Ausländerbehörde, otherwise letters are lost.

## Duldung (toleration) and ways to stay after rejection
- Duldung (§60a AufenthG): no residence permit, only a temporary suspension of deportation (e.g. no passport,
  illness, no flights). It is not a right to stay; it must be renewed often.
- "Duldung für Personen mit ungeklärter Identität" (§60b): worse status if a person does not help to get a passport.
- Ausbildungsduldung (vocational training) and Beschäftigungsduldung (stable employment): protection from deportation
  during training/work if conditions are met; can lead to a residence permit.
- §25a AufenthG: young people (under 27) who have lived in Germany for 3 years, went to school, integrate well.
- §25b AufenthG: long-term residents (generally 6 years, or 4 years with minor children) who are integrated and mostly
  support themselves.
- §19d AufenthG: residence permit for qualified tolerated people after training or work.
- Härtefallkommission (hardship commission) of each federal state: last-resort request in special cases.
- Kirchenasyl: churches sometimes give temporary shelter in hardship cases; it is not a legal right.
- Asylfolgeantrag (follow-up application, §71 AsylG): only with NEW facts or evidence, normally within 3 months
  of learning about them.

## Recognised refugees: next steps
- Apply in time for extension of the residence permit at the Ausländerbehörde (before it expires).
- Niederlassungserlaubnis (permanent residence): generally possible after 5 years (time of the asylum procedure counts),
  with German A2, livelihood mostly secured; for refugees faster (3 years) with C1 German and largely secured livelihood.
- BAMF can check later whether protection is still needed (Widerruf / Rücknahme). Travelling to the home country
  can lead to loss of protection — be very careful and get advice before any such trip.
- Family reunification for refugees: apply within 3 months of recognition for easier conditions (no proof of
  income/housing needed). A "fristwahrende Anzeige" (notice to keep the deadline) can be sent online/at the embassy.
- Naturalisation (Einbürgerung): generally after 5 years of legal residence with German B1 and livelihood;
  rules can change.

## Deportation (Abschiebung)
- Usually announced by the departure deadline in the decision. Night-time deportations happen.
- Detention before deportation (Abschiebungshaft) needs a court order — ask for a lawyer immediately.
- Voluntary return programmes exist (counselling at return advice centres) and may avoid an entry ban.
- An entry and residence ban (Einreise- und Aufenthaltsverbot) is usually given with deportation.

## Where to get free help
- Unabhängige Asylverfahrensberatung (independent asylum counselling, funded by the state) in reception centres
  and cities — Caritas, Diakonie, AWO, DRK and others.
- Flüchtlingsrat of each federal state (e.g. Flüchtlingsrat Berlin, Bayern, NRW).
- PRO ASYL (national organisation, information and lawyer contacts).
- Refugee Law Clinics at universities (free advice by law students under supervision).
- Lawyers for migration law (Fachanwalt für Migrationsrecht); ask about Beratungshilfe or Prozesskostenhilfe.
- Psychosocial centres for traumatised refugees (BAfF network).
- UNHCR Germany for information.
- In an emergency in Germany: 112 (ambulance/fire) and 110 (police).
