# Hannover — City Guide: History and Places to Visit

Note: opening times, prices and events change. Tell people to check the official website
(visit-hannover.com) or the venue before going.

## Quick facts
- Capital of the federal state of Lower Saxony (Niedersachsen), about 540,000 inhabitants
  (about 1.2 million in the Region Hannover).
- River: the Leine (and the smaller Ihme). The name comes from "Honovere", meaning "high bank" of the river.
- Known for: the Herrenhausen Gardens, the Maschsee lake, the Eilenriede city forest, Hannover Messe
  (one of the world's largest industrial trade fairs), and the UNESCO "City of Music" title (since 2014).
- Football club: Hannover 96 (stadium near the Maschsee).

## History in short
- Around 1150: first mention of the settlement "Honovere" on the Leine.
- 1241: Hannover receives town rights. Later a member of the Hanseatic League (trading towns).
- 1636: Duke Georg of Calenberg makes Hannover his residence. The court brings wealth and new buildings.
- 1676–1716: the famous philosopher and mathematician Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz works in Hannover
  as librarian and adviser. The university (Leibniz Universität) is named after him.
- 1692: Hannover becomes an Electorate (Kurfürstentum) of the Holy Roman Empire.
- 1714: Elector Georg Ludwig becomes King George I of Great Britain. From 1714 to 1837 Britain and
  Hannover share the same ruler (personal union). Electress Sophie, his mother, made the Herrenhausen
  gardens famous.
- 1814: Kingdom of Hannover.
- 1866: after the Austro-Prussian war Prussia annexes Hannover; it becomes a Prussian province.
- 1913: the New Town Hall (Neues Rathaus) is opened.
- 1943–1945: heavy bombing in the Second World War; most of the old city centre is destroyed.
  The ruined Aegidienkirche is kept as a war memorial.
- 1946: Hannover becomes the capital of the new state of Lower Saxony.
- 1947: the first Hannover trade fair (today's Hannover Messe).
- 2000: World Exhibition EXPO 2000 takes place in Hannover.

## Top places to visit
- Neues Rathaus (New Town Hall): huge, palace-like town hall from 1913 at the Maschpark. Inside are four
  city models showing Hannover in 1689, 1939, 1945 and today. A rare curved (inclined) lift goes up into the
  dome, with a great view over the city (small fee, check opening times).
- Herrenhäuser Gärten (Herrenhausen Gardens): one of the most important baroque gardens in Europe.
  - Großer Garten: formal baroque garden with hedges, statues, fountains, and the Great Fountain
    (Große Fontäne), one of the highest garden fountains in Europe. Also a grotto decorated by artist
    Niki de Saint Phalle. In summer: illuminations, fireworks competitions and the "Kleines Fest im Großen Garten".
  - Berggarten: botanical garden with greenhouses, orchids and cacti.
  - Georgengarten: free English-style landscape park, nice for walking and picnics.
  - Schloss Herrenhausen: rebuilt palace with a museum about the city's royal history.
  - Tram line 4 or 5 to "Herrenhäuser Gärten".
- Maschsee: large man-made lake (built 1934–1936) close to the centre, about 6 km walk around it.
  Boats, pedal boats, cafés, beach bar. Every summer the Maschseefest (big lake festival).
- Eilenriede: city forest of about 640 hectares, one of the largest city forests in Europe — bigger than
  Central Park in New York. Paths for walking, running and cycling.
- Altstadt (old town): rebuilt historic quarter with the Marktkirche (brick Gothic church, 14th century),
  the Altes Rathaus (old town hall), timber-framed houses in the Kramerstraße and Burgstraße, the
  Leibnizhaus and the Ballhof theatre. Flea market at the Leine bank on Saturdays.
- Leineschloss: former royal palace, today the seat of the Lower Saxony state parliament (Landtag).
- Nanas: three big colourful women sculptures by Niki de Saint Phalle at the Leibnizufer (1974), a symbol of the city.
- Aegidienkirche: church ruin and war memorial, with a peace bell given by Hannover's twin city Hiroshima.
- Der Rote Faden ("the red thread"): a red line painted on the pavement, about 4.2 km long, that leads
  past about 36 sights in the city centre. A booklet is available at the tourist information. Easy self-guided tour.
- Erlebnis-Zoo Hannover: popular zoo with themed worlds (Africa river, Indian jungle palace, Canadian
  Yukon Bay, a farm). Good for children; near the Eilenriede.
- Sea Life Hannover: aquarium in Herrenhausen.
- Lindener Berg and the Linden district: lively area with cafés, street art and a hill with a view.

## Museums
- Sprengel Museum: modern art (Picasso, Kurt Schwitters, Niki de Saint Phalle), at the Maschsee.
- Landesmuseum Hannover: natural history, archaeology, art and a vivarium with fish and reptiles.
- Museum August Kestner: art and objects from ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome.
- Historisches Museum Hannover: city history (it has been closed for renovation; check if it has reopened).
- Kestner Gesellschaft and Kunstverein: contemporary art exhibitions.

## Events through the year
- Hannover Messe (spring): giant industrial trade fair at the exhibition grounds (Messegelände).
- Schützenfest Hannover (late June / early July): one of the largest marksmen's festivals in the world, with a
  huge funfair and parade.
- Maschseefest (summer): food, music and stands around the lake.
- Kleines Fest im Großen Garten and fireworks competitions in Herrenhausen (summer).
- Christmas markets (late November to December) in the old town around the Marktkirche, including a
  "Finnish Christmas village".

## Getting around and tips
- Public transport: trams, buses and S-Bahn trains of the GVH network (operator Üstra). Day tickets and the
  HannoverCard (free public transport and discounts at sights) are available.
- Hannover Hauptbahnhof (main station) is right in the city centre; most sights are walking distance or a short tram ride.
- Tourist information: near the main station / Ernst-August-Platz (check current address).
- Family tip: the Georgengarten, Maschsee, Eilenriede and the Rote Faden walk are free.
